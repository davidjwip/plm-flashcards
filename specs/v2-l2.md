# v3 — Level 2 clusters, search, and the L2 chip

Status: **approved** (decisions D1–D5 confirmed by Dave); phase A in progress.
Non-goals are as binding as goals.

## 1. Problem

PLM Level 2 is not only single letters and vowels: the class also works on starting
clusters (`sm`, `bl`, `pl`, `sn` …), ending clusters (`nd`, `nt`, `nk`, `mp`, `st`, `ll`,
`ss` …), and `ar`/`or` in the middle of words. The bank has the vowels, the r-controlled
vowels and the Level 3 digraphs already, but **not one cluster tag** — so the app cannot
offer that work at all. With 46 cluster patterns added the pattern list reaches ~90 rows,
which is unusable as a flat checklist, and there is no single action for "practise
everything George is doing at Level 2".

## 2. Behaviour

| ID  | Behaviour                                                                                            |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| B1  | Every cluster is its own pattern, with position in the id: `st-` (start) vs `-st` (end).             |
| B2  | Every pattern carries at least **10 words** — thin sets are a data bug, not a UI state.              |
| B3  | Every word has a committed mp3 in `en-GB-SoniaNeural`; no runtime or browser speech for these sets.  |
| B4  | The pattern list is grouped and searchable; a grown-up can find `sm` without scrolling 90 rows.      |
| B5  | One **L2 chip** ticks the whole Level 2 set, including the thin three-letter clusters.               |
| B6  | The same L2 definition filters the spelling-bee deck, so bee practice matches what he can decode.    |
| B7  | Level 3 digraphs stay available but out of the L2 chip, except `ch`, `sh`, `th`, `ck`, which are in. |
| B8  | Nothing regresses: one-screen landing, fixed word frame, verdicts, party track, bee dictation.       |

### Ghost e (new, 1)

| id        | label                | words |
| --------- | -------------------- | ----- |
| `ghost_e` | ghost e (have, come) | 12    |

A final `e` that is **not pronounced and does not lengthen** the vowel before it — `have`,
`love`, `come`, `none`, `done`, `gone`, `some`, `give`, `glove`, `shove`, `above`, `twelve`.
`live`, `dove` and `one` were rejected: the first two are heteronyms whose other reading
teaches the opposite rule, and `one` has an unwritten /w/ so it cannot be sounded out. It is
the counterpart of the split digraph (`a_e`, `i_e`, `o_e`, `u_e`), where the `e` is silent but
**does** change the vowel. `are` and `were` also end in a silent `e` but their vowel is
r-controlled, so they are taught under `ar`/`er` instead of joining this set.

### The L2 set (what the chip ticks)

Short vowels `a e i o u` · long vowels `ee`, `oo`, `ou` · all 27 starting clusters · all 19
ending clusters · `ar`, `or` · `ch`, `sh`, `th`, `ck`, `ng` · `ghost_e`. **62 patterns.**
**Not** in the chip: the other long-vowel teams, diphthongs, `tch`, `schwa`,
`ear`/`eer`/`air`/`are`.

#### Level 2 means every sound in the word has been taught

The condition is coverage, not difficulty and not syllable count. `smart` is Level 2 material —
`sm` + `ar` + `t`, every grapheme taught, even though it is a cluster word. `armchair` is not,
because `air` has not been taught; `strawberry` is not, because of `aw`; `squawk` is not, because
of `aw`; `throne` is not, because of the split digraph `o_e`.

Level 2 therefore contains, for each word: short vowels and single consonants, the taught digraphs
(`ch` `sh` `th` `ck` `ng`), `ar`/`or`, `oo`/`ou`, `ghost_e`, and the taught clusters — and **nothing
else**. A word showing an untaught grapheme is out of the set no matter which taught pattern it also
shows.

Graphemes are found by a left-to-right longest-match segmenter, because substring matching lies:
`chair` is `ch|air`, never `ai|r`, and it is the `air` that disqualifies it.

Measured on the current bank: **465 of the 734 words that show an L2 pattern are coverage-clean**;
the other 269 contain at least one untaught grapheme. The cluster word lists are being rebuilt to
that rule.

A manual subset of patterns stays unfiltered — a grown-up who ticks `str-` alone may well want
`strawberry`, and they can see the word on screen before the child reads it.

Ten clusters cannot reach ten taught-only words at all: `scr-` 6, `spl-` 2, `spr-` 3, `str-` 6,
`shr-` 3, `thr-` 4, `squ-` 3, `-lp` 6, `-lf` 7, `-xt` 2. Whether they stay in the chip at their true
maximum or come out of the chip is **open** (Dave's call).

## 4. Word and tag requirements

- **10 words minimum per pattern**, with two documented exceptions where English has no
  tenth word: `-lp` (7: `help kelp gulp pulp yelp scalp whelp`) and `-xt` (6: `next text
context pretext hypertext subtext`). Verified against a 104k-word dictionary; the
  exceptions live in `scripts/validate-bank.mjs`, so they are enforced, not assumed.
- The data pass adds **354 curated words** (bank 861 → 1215) and retags 548 existing words,
  because one word can serve several clusters (`stamp` = `st-` + `-mp`, `blend` = `bl-` +
  `-nd`). Every new word is a real dictionary word, validated for spelling, cluster position
  and length before it is written.
- **Tags are generated from spelling by position**, then reviewed:
  - `X-` matches a word starting with `X` with at least one letter past the cluster
    (so `and`, `ask`, `cry`, `sky`, `old` all count — a rule requiring two letters past the
    cluster silently loses the easiest words);
  - `-X` matches a word ending with `X` with at least one letter before it;
  - a word carries **every** cluster it demonstrates, plus its vowel/r-controlled tags;
  - `ck`, `ng`, `ll`, `ss` are never produced as side effects of a cluster rule — they are
    their own patterns.
- **Existing thin patterns are topped up too**: `-st` (9), `-lt` (7), `-ld` (6) and the
  rest must each reach 10, not just the new ones.
- The bank validator's minimum rises from "≥1 word" to "**≥10 words**", so a thin set fails
  CI instead of quietly shipping.

## 5. Audio requirements

- Every word in the bank **and** in the bee deck gets an mp3 from
  `scripts/generate-audio.py`: `en-GB-SoniaNeural`, rate −8%, mono 22.05 kHz, 64 kbps,
  loudness −14 LUFS.
- The generator currently reads only `data/wordbank.json`; it must also cover
  `data/bee.json` so bee words are never spoken by the browser.
- New words are not merged until their audio is committed — a word without a file is a
  word that speaks in whatever voice the tablet has, which is exactly what Dave ruled out.
- `audio-manifest.json` (and the app's `hasFile` check) must list every word.

## 6. Search and grouping

- The panel's toolbar holds the search field and the **L2 chip**. Not the header: it
  already carries three controls and must never wrap (AC55 holds at 360px).
- The panel groups rows, in this order: **Short vowels · Long vowels · R-controlled ·
  Digraphs · Starting clusters · Ending clusters · Silent e**. Groups collapse; the open ones
  remember state. `ghost_e` is the only member of Silent e.
- A search field filters as you type, on label **or** id (`sm`, "start", "short a").
  - Selection survives filtering — a ticked row that is filtered out stays ticked.
  - The header pill keeps showing the true selected count.
  - No match shows "no patterns match …", not an empty box.
  - Clearing the field restores the full grouped list; focus lands in the field when the
    panel opens.

## 7. Bee Level 2

- The bee panel carries a **Level 2** switch beside the three level boxes; the header is unchanged
  in bee mode.
- With it on, the deck **is** the Sound patterns Level 2 set — the same words, built by the same
  `runDictionary()` — so the two modes can never disagree about what Level 2 contains. The count
  line shows that number, and it matches what the chip reports in Sound patterns.
- The three source boxes (Cambridge Starters, Cambridge Movers, nonsense) do not apply while
  Level 2 is on: Level 2 replaces them. The panel disables them and says so, rather than leaving a
  grown-up to discover that unticking them emptied the deck.
- With Level 2 off, the deck is the Cambridge deck as before; an empty source selection says
  `no source levels selected` and clears the round, so no word outlives the deck that produced it.
- Consequence: ghost-e words (`have`, `come`) **are** in the bee Level 2 deck, because they are in
  the curated Level 2 set. Under the earlier filter model they were excluded for being unprovable
  from spelling; with a mirror the word list is the authority, not a spelling heuristic.

## 11. Decisions

| #   | Decision                                           | Alternative rejected                                                                               |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| D1  | one pattern per cluster, position in the id        | family-only tags — Dave wants "this week it's `sm` and `sn`"                                       |
| D2  | `ch`/`sh`/`th`/`ck`/`ng` in the L2 chip; `tch` out | including every digraph — `tch` is a spelling of `ch` the class has not met yet                    |
| D3  | `shr-`/`spl-` in the chip even though rare         | holding thin clusters back — Dave's call: the L2 chip is the class list, not the well-stocked list |
